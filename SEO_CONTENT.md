## What is Skin Tone & Pigment Visibility Matcher?

The Skin Tone & Pigment Visibility Matcher is a free interactive tool that shows how nine tattoo ink colours perform across all six Fitzpatrick skin types. Users select a skin type from the visual grid, and the tool displays visibility ratings, fade rate scores, and practical artist notes for each ink colour.

The tool evaluates Black, Grey/Black & Grey, White, Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, and Purple inks. Each colour receives a visibility score from 1 (Poor) to 5 (Excellent) and a fade rate score from 1 (slowest fade) to 5 (fastest fade), specific to the selected Fitzpatrick type. Accompanying text notes explain real-world behaviour, such as white ink fading to near-invisible on Type V skin or red pigment shifting orange on Type IV skin.

The tool draws on clinical and artist literature rather than brand-specific testing. It serves as a planning reference for artists and an educational resource for clients considering colour tattoos.

## Who Should Use This

**Tattoo artists**, Use during consultations to recommend ink colours that will maintain visibility on a client's specific skin type. Reference the fade rate scores to set realistic expectations about touch-up needs.

**Tattoo clients**, Understand how your chosen colours will look immediately after healing and how they will age over years. Learn which pigments are likely to fade fastest on your skin type.

**Tattoo apprentices**, Study pigment behaviour across different skin tones before working with real clients. Build colour theory knowledge specific to tattoo application.

**Studio managers**, Share the tool with clients during the booking process to reduce consultation time and improve informed decision-making.

## How to Use

1. **Select your Fitzpatrick skin type**, Click one of the six buttons in the skin selector grid. Each button shows a colour swatch, type number, and description (Very Fair, Fair, Medium, Olive, Brown, Deep).

2. **View the pigment performance results**, After selection, the results section appears below the skin selector. A summary text explains general pigment behaviour for that skin type.

3. **Read individual ink cards**, Each of the nine ink colours displays:
   - Colour swatch and name
   - Visibility badge (Poor, Weak, Moderate, Good, Excellent)
   - Visibility bar (1–5 scale)
   - Fade rate bar (1–5 scale, where 5 = fastest fade)
   - Artist note with specific guidance

4. **Compare across skin types**, Click different skin type buttons to see how the same ink colour performs differently. The page scrolls smoothly to the results section each time.

5. **Apply the information**, Use the visibility and fade rate scores alongside the artist notes to make informed colour decisions for tattoo designs.

## Use Case Examples

### Example 1: Client with Fitzpatrick Type VI (Deep) wants a colour tattoo

**Tool input:** Select Type VI in the skin grid.

**Tool output:** Black ink shows visibility 3/5 (textural contrast rather than colour contrast). White ink shows 1/5 visibility with note "Not recommended under any circumstances." Red ink shows 1/5 visibility with note "Near-invisible after healing in most cases." Blue ink shows 2/5 visibility, navy and dark cobalt retain some contrast.

**Application:** The artist explains that only high-contrast pigments retain long-term visibility on deep skin. They recommend a blackwork design with possible dark blue accents, and advise against coloured work, especially warm tones. The client understands realistic expectations before booking.

### Example 2: Artist planning white ink highlights on Fitzpatrick Type II (Fair)

**Tool input:** Select Type II in the skin grid.

**Tool output:** White ink shows visibility 3/5 with note "Subtle highlight and accent use only. Near-invisible in most clients after 3–5 years." Fade rate shows 5/5 (fastest fade).

**Application:** The artist uses white ink only as a highlight accent in a larger black-and-grey piece. They inform the client that the white areas will require touch-ups within 3–5 years. They avoid using white as a standalone element.

### Example 3: Client with Fitzpatrick Type IV (Olive) considering red ink

**Tool input:** Select Type IV in the skin grid.

**Tool output:** Red ink shows visibility 3/5 with note "Risk of orange-shift as skin warmth neutralises red. Vibrancy fades quickly." Fade rate shows 4/5.

**Application:** The artist discusses the orange-shift risk with the client. They suggest using red only in areas with high saturation and deep packing, and recommend a darker red formulation. They schedule a patch test due to red pigment's higher allergenic potential. The client decides to use red sparingly in the design.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1: What Fitzpatrick skin types does the tool cover?**
The tool covers all six Fitzpatrick skin types: Type I (Very fair), Type II (Fair), Type III (Medium), Type IV (Olive/Light brown), Type V (Brown), and Type VI (Deep/Dark brown to black).

**Q2: How many ink colours does the tool evaluate?**
The tool evaluates nine ink colour families: Black, Grey/Black & Grey, White, Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, and Purple.

**Q3: What do the visibility ratings mean?**
Visibility ratings range from 1 (Poor) to 5 (Excellent). They indicate how clearly the pigment will appear against the selected skin type immediately after healing, based on clinical and artist literature.

**Q4: What do the fade rate ratings mean?**
Fade rate ratings range from 1 to 5, where 1 indicates the slowest fade (most durable) and 5 indicates the fastest fade (least durable). Higher numbers mean the colour will lose visibility more quickly over time.

**Q5: Does the tool account for different ink brands?**
No. The ratings reflect typical pigment behaviour across skin types based on clinical and artist literature. Individual results vary with ink brand, needle depth, aftercare, UV exposure, and laser history.

**Q6: Is white ink ever recommended for dark skin?**
The tool shows white ink as ineffective on Fitzpatrick Types V and VI, with visibility ratings of 1/5. For Type IV, white ink is described as "near-invisible after healing." The tool recommends against white ink on deep skin tones under most circumstances.

**Q7: What is the best ink colour for long-term durability across all skin types?**
Black ink consistently receives the highest visibility ratings and lowest fade rates across all six skin types. The tool describes black as "the gold standard for longevity" on Type I skin and notes it remains visible even on Type VI skin as textural contrast.

**Q8: Can I use this tool to predict exactly how my tattoo will heal?**
No. The tool provides general guidance based on typical pigment behaviour. Individual healing varies based on ink brand, application technique, aftercare, sun exposure, and individual skin chemistry. Always consult your artist for personalised recommendations.

**Q9: Does the tool include neon or specialty ink colours?**
No. The tool covers nine standard ink colour families. Neon and specialty pigments are not included in the current version.

**Q10: How should artists use this tool with clients?**
Artists can use the tool during consultations to show clients how different colours perform on their specific skin type. The visibility and fade rate scores help set realistic expectations, and the artist notes provide practical guidance for colour selection.
